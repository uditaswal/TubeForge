# Storyboard and asset direction

Use for a visual treatment or asset plan from an accepted script. Read the project contract and docs/SHORT_FORM_VIDEO_RULEBOOK.md first. This skill supplies planning guidance only; use existing TubeForge integrations for asset creation. Keep yt-script as the sole narration/hook/scene-direction authority. Do not generate replacement narration here or change providers, formats, approvals or channel voice.

1. Extract the supplied audience, purpose, platform, format, duration, tone, references, constraints and desired viewer takeaway. Mark inferred and missing values. Inspect supplied source material before describing it; filenames are not evidence. Ask only for material missing information.
2. Bind the plan to accepted script and scene hashes. Preserve the accepted scene contract, word anchors, reveal order, layouts and render identity. Propose scene changes as revisions requiring the existing exact-artifact review gate; do not silently apply them.
3. Give every scene a concrete teaching or storytelling purpose. Select an opening visual, a reveal and a closing payoff tied to the brief. Prefer real evidence for factual demonstrations; distinguish illustrative generated imagery from documentary footage.
4. Describe subject and identity anchors; ordered subject actions; setting and lighting; shot size, position and depth; and camera behavior. Separate title/caption overlays from scene geometry. Use camera movement only when it helps meaning. Differentiate a physical dolly from a lens zoom.
5. Choose source footage, licensed stock, generated stills, diagrams or generated motion according to scene needs and available approved tools. Use real diagrams for exact quantities and relationships. Do not invent tool availability or use paid providers without run-specific authorization. Record an explicit fallback; surface any material quality downgrade for review.
6. Search stock with subject, action and viewpoint; inspect relevance, crop, resolution, duration, watermark and usage rights before acceptance. Record the actual asset license and evidence; do not assume a provider makes every asset unrestricted.
7. Vary compositions where the story benefits; avoid an entire video of identical headline cards or decorative motion. Preserve palette, typography, character identity and readable hierarchy across changes.

Return an ordered scene table with scene ID, accepted hash/word anchors, timing, source claim, visual purpose, subject, action, framing, camera, overlays, asset choice, source/license evidence, continuity constraints, fallback and unresolved issues. Explicitly label unavailable inputs.

Acceptance: every accepted narration beat has a grounded visual purpose; inspect the real storyboard manifest and contact sheet tied to accepted hashes. Check phone-size readability and topic-specific teaching value. A written plan alone does not establish creative acceptance or a completed render.
